# Visual direction

**Working hypothesis.** The first creative brief can override these choices; document durable changes in [experiments.md](experiments.md).

## Visual identity

Aim for illustrated, cinematic fantasy with simplified shapes and clear silhouettes. Mood: mysterious and tense rather than graphically violent. Use a restrained base of charcoal `#151B25`, slate `#425466`, parchment `#E8DFC9`, and one warm accent `#E7A94B`; reserve muted red `#BD5353` for danger. This palette is a starting point, not a mandatory color filter.

Keep the subject and immediate survival problem readable at phone size. Use a mix of establishing views, character/action closeups and simple diagrams when they clarify geography, inventory or cause and effect. Diagram labels should add information rather than repeat every caption. Maintain visual consistency in character silhouette, clothing, props, location, lighting and inventory from shot to shot; record reference descriptions and asset variants in the brief.

## Composition and motion

- Design for 9:16; the parent project already uses 1080 × 1920. Keep critical text, faces and diagram labels away from edges and areas where app controls or captions could cover them. Test on each intended platform's actual preview; overlay positions vary.
- Establish an unobstructed caption region per episode. Check text and small diagrams at phone size; use contrast, a dark backing or shadow where necessary. Never rely on color alone to communicate a danger or state.
- Use motion to reveal geography, attention or cause and effect: purposeful pans, scale changes and staged diagram reveals. Avoid constant camera movement, flashes and transitions that obscure the action. Respect reduced sensory intensity when possible.
- Show the consequence of the character's action; do not let unrelated generated imagery contradict narration. Include breathing room for details viewers must read.

## Assets and consistency

Prefer editable Remotion shapes, text and diagrams for information that must remain accurate; original generated illustrations can supply atmosphere and character art. Choose whichever assets the specific episode needs. Record prompts, model/tool, creation date, selected outputs, rights and any edits in the episode brief. Do not promise identical character generations: verify each shot and repair or replace inconsistent assets. Keep source assets separate from render output and credit where required.

## Frame review

Review the opening, every scene transition, caption-heavy moments, the consequence and the final frame in Studio. Check for clipping, spelling errors, unreadable text, inconsistent characters and misleading diagrams. A still-frame pass supplements, but does not replace, a real-time audiovisual review.
