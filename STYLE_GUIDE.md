# Style guide

Give viewers an unbelievable survival experience in an imagined world. Show the danger, the character’s choice and its consequence clearly enough to follow without prior episodes. Choose a point of view that serves the premise. Treat continuity as established only when the episode brief explicitly adopts it.

## Picture and pacing

- Build each shot from illustrated raster plates. Use transparent poses or foreground pieces when a scene needs independent movement. In Remotion, use camera movement, controlled lighting, cuts and the occasional impact cue to direct attention. Keep drawn characters and environments in image assets.
- Use a simple silhouette, readable action and a strong point of focus at 270 × 480. In a dim shot, use a window, dashboard or streetlight to separate the important shape from its surroundings. Leave room for Indonesian captions and check against the intended platform’s preview.
- Draw the character’s neutral pose, action and reaction using consistent proportions, costume and lighting. In seated views, check how the jaw, neck and shoulders meet and how the body contacts the seat. Use a motivated cut for a large pose change. Scrub the critical transition and replay it at normal speed.
- Show a constraint through the scene itself. In *Kemacetan Terakhir*, the cars must visibly block the driver, and a hand on the headrest must establish where the sound comes from before the reveal. Use the frame that shows the action to place its sound cue.

## Narration, captions and sound

- Write natural conversational Bahasa Indonesia. Keep the delivery masculine, mature and firm, with clear articulation and deliberate pauses. The [selected v4 voice registry](voice-samples-v4/README.md) holds the working settings. Use the owner’s recording when supplied and keep a clean master.
- Caption short spoken phrases with timings from the final narration. Read the text at phone size and listen across each boundary. Use emphasis sparingly on words that carry the beat.
- Start with recorded location tone and foley, then add a restrained tension cue where it serves the scene. Leave room for the narration and let a quiet beat hold before a knock or reveal. Use the shared recordings or add new assets with an explicit redistribution license and source credit.

## Finish a cut

Keep one brief, script, asset record and review. Use the brief to specify the intended viewer response, the review to record observable findings, and the decision log when feedback or other evidence changes the workflow. Record asset provenance, voice rights and any required credit before release. Check the actual platform preview and current disclosure rules when publishing is authorized.
