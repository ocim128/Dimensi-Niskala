# Creative thesis

**Status: initial hypothesis, 2026-11-18.** Test with actual episodes and feedback; revise when evidence or the owner's preferences justify it.

## Audience promise

Give viewers an unbelievable experience of survival in an imagined world. A clear danger, limited resources, a consequential choice and a satisfying change in the situation are our starting hypothesis for delivering that feeling. The viewer should understand both the problem and why the choice matters even without seeing previous episodes.

## Editorial pillars

1. **A specific survival problem:** shelter, water, warmth, food, navigation, trust or a fictional threat. Make the constraint visible.
2. **Fantasy with rules:** introduce one understandable supernatural rule, cost or creature behavior that affects the choice. Avoid an unexplained solution.
3. **Human stakes:** center a character's need or relationship, not just a list of mechanics.
4. **Cause and consequence:** show an attempt and its result. A small unresolved question can invite another episode, but this episode must still pay off.

The owner has not chosen a storytelling format. Experiment with an immersive survivor diary, a cinematic story, a viewer-facing scenario or an in-world explanation; choose the approach that best serves each episode, then learn from feedback. Do not present fantasy survival mechanics as reliable real-world emergency advice. For any real-world factual claim, verify with credible sources or make it explicitly fictional.

Possible approaches to test, not a production queue or established series:

- **Survivor diary:** experience an impossible world through a character's immediate choices and observations.
- **Viewer scenario:** invite the viewer to imagine facing a strange rule, then reveal the consequence of a decision.
- **Cinematic miniature:** tell a short story with visual discovery and sparse narration, emphasizing wonder and payoff.

For each premise, identify the specific moment that should make someone feel wonder, shock or curiosity. Then show enough of the situation for that moment to make sense; “unbelievable” should be a viewer experience, not a promise of exaggerated claims.

## Episode shape

Use a flexible structure, not a fixed script: open with the immediate threat or intriguing constraint; establish the situation and the relevant world rule; show an attempted solution and a complication; resolve the immediate question and leave an earned next question if useful. The first seconds should communicate a reason to keep watching. Choose duration to fit the story; roughly 30–75 seconds is an initial planning range, not a requirement or platform limit.

**Illustrative premise, not canon:** A traveler discovers that a ruined tower provides warmth only while its bell remains silent. When a storm arrives, they must choose between shelter and warning a stranger. A visible choice and consequence matter more than the exact idea.

## Originality and continuity

Create original settings, characters, scripts, imagery and sound or use properly licensed assets. Study the reference for general storytelling techniques; do not trace its shots, copy its script, characters, design, music or exact premise. Do not imply affiliation with an existing franchise. Establish canon only when a brief explicitly adopts it; track the surviving character, inventory, location and unresolved threads in that brief and update after review. An initial episode may stand alone without a series bible.

## What we are testing

- **Comprehension:** can a viewer name the threat, choice and outcome on one viewing?
- **Wonder:** what moment felt surprising, impossible or memorable, and why?
- **Interest:** do viewers stay through the decision and ask relevant questions?
- **Distinctiveness:** does the fantasy rule change survival behavior rather than decorate a generic scenario?
- **Production viability:** can the agent produce a coherent, reviewable episode with available tools and assets?

Use [feedback-loop.md](feedback-loop.md) to test these assumptions. Never treat a single video's views as proof of the whole thesis.
