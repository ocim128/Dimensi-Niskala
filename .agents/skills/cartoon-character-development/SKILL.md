# Cartoon character development

Start from the episode brief and any owner feedback. Match the chosen style and existing character references. In this project, follow the reference's simplified cartoon style when the brief asks for it. Distinctive, appealing shape and a coherent pose matter at least as much as a technically smooth animation.

## Design before rigging

Create or adapt a character sheet appropriate to the episode. Include the views and poses the story actually needs, with the head, neck, collar, shoulders and torso visible together. When the character is seated, include the seat and relevant contact points. Compare proportions, silhouette, facial identity, costume and light direction across views. Check both a large image and a phone-size presentation. Repair mismatched or awkward shapes before extending them through the video.

The owner's attempt 3 feedback identified a long, narrow neck that made the seated character look wrong. Inspect the transition from jaw through neck into shoulders and clothing, while retaining the deliberate proportions of the chosen cartoon style. Use the episode's feedback and references for other characters rather than applying a fixed anatomy template.

## Test the performance

Build a short representative test in the actual production setup, such as a neutral seated hold, a look and a reach. Give each movement a clear starting pose, action and resulting pose. Inspect the critical pose transitions frame by frame, then play the motion at normal speed and phone size. Look for identity changes, sliding joints, broken seat contact and abrupt head flips. Revise the drawing as well as the rig when the static design looks wrong.

Choose the simplest technique that can produce the approved visual result, such as distinct drawn poses, layered artwork, a rig or a motivated camera cut. A generated design sheet is a visual reference, not automatically a consistent or editable animation asset. If generating raster concepts, use the available image-generation skill and record the selected asset and prompt in the episode handoff. Keep Remotion as the composition and timing environment when the episode brief calls for it.

Record the sheet and test paths, replay interval, observations and any remaining visual issue in the episode handoff. Obtain owner feedback when appropriate and identify unreviewed judgments honestly.
